- **Command(s):** none yet; this note defines the study. Commands that produce results are recorded in `view_signal.md`.
- **Datasets:** STL-10 ([canonical](https://cs.stanford.edu/~acoates/stl10/), [HF `tanganke/stl10`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tanganke/stl10); canonical archive used) `train` as the clean labeled bank and `test` as the clean twins; STL-10-C built locally from STL-10 `test` (10 corruptions x 5 severities; no HF mirror).
- **Backbone:** ResNet-18 from scratch ([HF architecture reference](https://huggingface.co/timm/resnet18.a1_in1k), weights not used); `last.pt` of `radius_marginal_lam{0.02,0.05,0.1}_s{0,1,2}` (Variant A).
- **Scope:** h is not analyzed; LeJEPA's product is z. z only.

## Question

E6 and the shift study (`research_notes/radius_transfer`, `research_notes/radius_usage/shift_summary.md`) showed that the radius moves under corruption while frozen readers do not use it. That leaves open whether the *displacement* of an embedding under corruption contains a radial component that carries a usable signal about the corruption (not about the class): how much of the clean-to-corrupted displacement is radial, would restoring the clean radius recover accuracy, does the radius residual tell the corruption severity beyond what the direction tells, and does weighting the views of an image by their radius improve multi-view aggregation. A positive answer to the last three would be evidence against "the radius is a nuisance"; a negative answer supports "no usable view-quality signal". The class-information claim of the program is not retested here.

## Common setup

Runs: the nine sweep runs. Bank: clean STL-10 `train` center-view z. Test: the 8000 clean STL-10 `test` images and their 50 STL-10-C twins (cells = corruption x severity). Readers (frozen protocol, fitted on the clean bank only): Euclidean k-NN, linear probe, MLP, as in `randomization.make_fit` (k = 20, MLP weight decay chosen on the clean bank). Statistics: paired bootstrap over test images within each training seed (the same resample for every comparison of a seed), averaged over the three seeds, 1000 replicates, 90% and 95% percentile intervals, verdicts `equivalent` / `different` / `inconclusive` by `randomization.verdict`. Seed variability beyond three seeds and the dependence among severities of one corruption (shared images) are not modeled. Margins, fixed here: **0.5 accuracy points** for accuracies; **0.01 Spearman** (the analogue of the 0.01 AUROC margin of `ood_radius`) for severity-prediction scores; the decomposition (part A) is descriptive and has no margin.

Notation: `z = r u`, `rho = log r`; `z_c`, `z_x` are the clean and corrupted center-view embeddings of the same image; `f` is the clean-fitted direction -> log-radius kernel ridge map of E6 / `shift` (out-of-fold on the bank, fitted on the whole bank for test images) and `res = rho - f(u)` the radius residual.

## Part A: radial vs tangential displacement

For each image, `D = z_x - z_c`, `a = <D, u_c>` (radial component along the clean direction) and `t = ||D - a u_c||` (tangential). Reported per cell, per corruption and overall: `F_rad = sum a^2 / sum (a^2 + t^2)` (ratio of sums over images) with a bootstrap 95% interval, the median `r_x / r_c`, and the same `F_rad` for a null displacement between each clean image and a *different* random clean image (a fixed permutation), which shows what the radial share of a pure content change is. For orientation, a displacement in a random direction has `F_rad` about `1/256`.

Predictions (written before looking): (A1) corruption displacements are mostly tangential: `F_rad < 0.10` for most corruptions, at all severities, for all lambdas. (A2) `F_rad` grows with severity for the corruptions that E6 found to shift the radius strongly (noise, blur, JPEG) but stays below 0.5. (A3) The clean-vs-other-image null has a radial share of similar order to the weak corruptions. These are descriptive readings: `F_rad >= 0.10` is called "not negligible", `>= 0.5` "mostly radial".

## Part B: oracle radius

Four conditions on the corrupted test embeddings of every cell, readers frozen on the clean bank: `baseline` (`z_x`), `oracle radius` (`r_c u_x`: corrupted direction, clean twin's radius), `oracle direction` (`r_x u_c`: clean direction, corrupted radius) and `scaled constant` (`median bank radius * u_x`; the post-hoc variant of the shift study, here pre-registered). Comparisons (difference in accuracy points, paired by image): `oracle radius - baseline`, `scaled constant - baseline`, `oracle direction - clean twin accuracy` and `baseline - clean twin accuracy` (the total corruption loss). `Recovery = (oracle radius - baseline) / (clean - baseline)` at the corruption and overall level, bootstrap interval from the same resamples (reported only where `clean - baseline` exceeds 1 point on average).

Predictions: (B1) `oracle radius - baseline` is `equivalent` (within 0.5 points) overall for k-NN and MLP, recovery CI includes 0 and stays below 0.10. (B2) For the linear probe a small non-zero effect is possible because of the bias / scale mechanism found in the frozen-linear study (|effect| below 1 point); I expect `different` for the linear probe at most in the direction "oracle radius helps". (B3) `oracle direction` is within 0.5 points of the clean accuracy overall (the shifted radius alone does not hurt). (B4) `scaled constant - baseline` is `equivalent` (as in the shift study). If B1 fails for any reader (an oracle radius recovers more than 0.5 points), the radius carries recoverable corruption-specific information for that reader, against the nuisance claim; I will report it as such.

## Part C: corruption severity from the radius residual

Per corruption: rows are the 8000 clean twins (severity 0) and the 8000 corrupted images of each severity 1..5 (48,000 rows; the six rows of an image share an `image_id`). Scores: (C1) signed `res` alone: Spearman correlation with severity (sign reported; no orientation is chosen afterwards); (C2) held-out predictions of severity from ridge regression (ridge penalty by `RidgeCV` over `1e-1..1e3`, 5-fold cross-validation grouped by image) on the standardized direction `u` ("direction model"), on `[res, |res|, rho]` ("radius model") and on both ("direction + radius"). Held-out Spearman between the predicted and the true severity per model; the effect of interest is `Spearman(direction + radius) - Spearman(direction)`, paired bootstrap over images (a resample of images keeps the six rows together), margin 0.01 (`does not add` if the 90% interval is inside +-0.01, `adds` if the 95% interval's lower end is above 0.01, `worse` if the upper end is below -0.01, `inconclusive` otherwise).

Predictions: (C1) `|Spearman(res, severity)|` is at least 0.3 for corruptions with a strong radius shift in E6 (noise, blur, JPEG) and below 0.3 for most others. (C2) The direction model predicts severity much better than the radius model for every corruption (direction Spearman above radius Spearman), and `direction + radius` does not add more than 0.01 for most corruptions; I expect it to `add` for a minority (the same strongly shifting corruptions). A result in which radius adds for most corruptions would count against the nuisance framing for corruption detection.

## Part D: radius-weighted multi-view aggregation

Each test image has K = 8 augmented views (views 1..8 of the embedding dumps; the center view is excluded) of the clean or corrupted image. An image-level embedding is formed by one of the schemes below, as a unit vector scaled to the median bank radius (so that readers see a direction at the scale of the bank; the bank is the clean center-view directions at the same scale, i.e. the `constant radius` training of E1): `mean` (normalized mean of the unit directions, the reference), `radius-weighted` (weights proportional to `r_v`; equivalently the normalized mean of the raw `z_v`), `inverse radius` (weights proportional to `1/r_v`), `residual-weighted` (weights proportional to `exp(-|res_v| / s)` with `s` the standard deviation of the bank's held-out residuals), `top radius view` (the single view with the largest `r_v`), `bottom radius view` (the single view with the smallest `r_v`) and, as context only, the center view alone. The first three weightings are mild (the relative spread of the radius is 0.17 to 0.23, so weights stay within about +-30% and the effective number of views is near 8; it is reported), the last two are the strong tests of whether the radius ranks view quality. Readers: Euclidean k-NN, linear probe, MLP, fitted on the bank directions; evaluated on every cell and on the clean test set. Comparisons (accuracy points, paired by image): every scheme vs `mean`, and `top radius view - bottom radius view`. Retrieval is not run in part D (classification only; deviation from the original idea list, for cost).

Predictions: (D1) `radius-weighted - mean` and `inverse radius - mean` are `equivalent` (within 0.5 points) for every reader on clean and corrupted images overall. (D2) `residual-weighted - mean` is `equivalent` or within -1 point. (D3) `top radius view - bottom radius view` is `equivalent` (the radius does not rank which view is the more informative), and both single-view schemes are `different` (lower) from `mean`. If `top - bottom` is `different` with a positive sign, or any weighting beats `mean` by more than 0.5 points with a CI above 0, the radius carries a view-quality signal and the nuisance claim is weakened.
