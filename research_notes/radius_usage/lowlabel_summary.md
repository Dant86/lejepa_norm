## Question

If the radius carried a confidence-like signal, it should matter most when labels are scarce, because a reader with little labeled data has little else to go on. Pre-stated hypothesis: randomizing the radius of z hurts the E1 readers more at 50-500 labels than with all 5000 labels. This note is the low-label repeat of E1 (`research_notes/radius_randomization/`) for lambda in {0.02, 0.05, 0.1}, three training seeds each; the cross-lambda reading is in `lowlabel_summary.md`.

## Setup (deviations from the base config)

Deviations from `configs/base.yaml`: none (k-NN `k` is capped by the per-class count, see below; 1000 bootstrap replicates, 5 shuffle repeats per randomized condition, margin 0.5 points = `eval.equivalence_margin`, 10 subsets = `eval.lowlabel_subsets`). Design, fixed in the code (`lejepa_norm.eval.lowlabel`) before any condition effect was looked at:

- **Budgets** (per class / total labels / share of the 5000-image labeled split): 5 / 50 / 1%, 10 / 100 / 2%, 50 / 500 / 10%, 500 / 5000 / 100% (the whole split; its single "subset" is the E1 setting).
- **Subsets:** 10 independent class-balanced random draws per budget (1 at 5000 labels). The draws depend only on the budget and the draw index, so every one of the 9 runs and every condition sees the same labeled images (paired). Subset-to-subset spread is reported (`subset SE`, `sd over subsets`).
- **Conditions, protocols, statistics:** exactly E1 (`lejepa_norm.eval.randomization`, same code path: `condition_cells`, `compare`): baseline, permute radius across images, residual-only shuffle, constant radius (unit norm); *retrained* and *frozen* protocols; paired bootstrap over test images within each seed, averaged over 3 seeds; TOST-style verdict with margin 0.5 points.
- **Readers:** Euclidean k-NN, linear probe, one-hidden-layer MLP (cosine k-NN dropped: radius-invariant by construction).
- **Hyperparameters** (decided before looking at condition effects): selected per subset on the *clean* labeled subset by stratified cross-validation inside the subset (`min(5, per-class count)` folds), then reused for all conditions and protocols. Linear probe `C` in {0.01, 0.1, 1}; MLP weight decay in {1e-4, 1e-2, 1} (the E1 grid); k-NN `k = min(20, per-class count)` (5 with 5 labels per class, 10 with 10, 20 beyond). At 5000 labels the cross-validation runs on the whole split (E1 used a single 20% validation split for the MLP; the linear probe used `C = 1`), so that budget is not bit-identical to E1.
- **Residual shuffle:** the direction -> log-radius regression uses no labels, so it is fitted once per run on the whole unlabeled `train` split (out-of-fold for the bank, E1 code) and the subset's images take their predictions from it. Fitting it on 50 images would be meaningless and would confound the condition with the budget.
- **Subset noise in the verdict:** the bootstrap interval conditions on the drawn subsets. `subset SE` is the standard error over subsets of the mean paired difference (combined over seeds); `verdict (with subsets)` adds it in quadrature to the bootstrap standard error (normal approximation). Because each subset's difference is measured on all test images, the subset SE also contains some test-image noise, i.e. it over-states the subset noise slightly: the second verdict is the stricter one. Training-seed variability beyond averaging 3 seeds is not covered (as in E1).

## Interpretation

What the data support (all numbers from the compact view above; points; `E/E` = equivalent under both verdicts):

1. **The hypothesis is not supported for the learned readers.** For the linear probe and the MLP the radius randomizations change accuracy by at most a few tenths of a point at every budget and lambda: every retrained-protocol cell of the residual shuffle is `E/E` (|difference| <= 0.07 points), and the permute and constant cells never reduce accuracy by more than the margin. There is no sign that these readers use the radius more when labels are scarce. Some low-label cells are `different`, but in the *positive* direction (removing the radius slightly helps: linear/constant +0.4 at 50 labels for lambda 0.02 and 0.05, MLP/permute +0.38 to +0.45 and MLP/constant +0.57 to +0.71 at 50 labels, mostly just above the margin), so they do not say the radius carries label information.
2. **Euclidean k-NN is the exception, and the sign is the opposite of "the radius is used".** With the radius removed (unit norm, retrained) k-NN *gains* accuracy when labels are scarce: +1.54 / +3.45 / +6.49 points at 50 labels for lambda 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.1 (verdict `different` both ways), +0.56 / +2.10 / +4.30 at 100 labels, -0.34 to +0.66 at 500 labels, and slightly *negative* (-0.34 to -0.42, `different`) with all 5000 labels (the E1 setting; E1 found -0.38 / -0.35 / -0.42). Euclidean distances on raw z mix the radius into the neighbour ranking; with few labeled neighbours that is noise that can be removed, with many neighbours the radius-direction coupling helps slightly. The gain at 50 labels grows with lambda (`kappa_z` falls from 26 to 15) while the baseline k-NN accuracy at 50 labels falls (0.609, 0.585, 0.535 for lambda 0.02, 0.05, 0.1), so the trend may partly reflect headroom; this is not disentangled here.
3. **Permuting the radius across images hurts Euclidean k-NN when retrained** (-1.67 / -2.16 / -1.70 at 50 labels, `D/D`; -0.4 to -0.9 at 100; -0.2 to -0.7 at 500; -0.45 to -0.70 at 5000), while the residual-only shuffle is neutral (`E/E`) for lambda 0.02 and 0.05 at every budget. So what the k-NN geometry is sensitive to is the part of the radius that is *predictable from the direction* (the radius-direction coupling), not the direction-independent part; together with point 2 this says the Euclidean k-NN readout is perturbed by changes of the radius-direction relation in either direction, which is a property of the readout metric, not evidence of label information in the radius. At lambda 0.1 the residual shuffle *raises* k-NN accuracy (+0.93 at 50 labels, +0.42 at 100, both `D/D`), again the sign of "the unpredictable radius part is noise".
4. **Frozen protocol** (constant radius excluded: chance level): equivalent in nearly all cells (|difference| <= 0.41). Exceptions (`D` in at least one verdict): k-NN permute at 50 labels, lambda 0.05 (-0.38, `E/D`) and lambda 0.1 (-0.75, `D/D`), and the linear probe at 5000 labels, lambda 0.02 (-0.41, `D/D`; E1 reported the same cell as -0.44, `different`).
5. **Subset noise:** the baseline accuracy itself varies a lot between labeled subsets (sd about 2.2-4.7 points at 50 labels, 1.2-2.1 at 100, 0.5-0.8 at 500; accuracy tables in the per-lambda notes), but the paired difference varies little (subset SE <= 0.63 points in the generated tables, excluding the frozen constant-radius cells at chance), so the verdicts are mostly stable. Adding it turns 8 `equivalent` verdicts into `different` (`E/D`) and one `different` into `inconclusive` (`D/I`); all of these have |difference| between 0.34 and 0.43 points, i.e. below the margin of 0.5 but with an interval that reaches it once subset noise is added (points 1-4).

Caveats: three training seeds per lambda; the intervals are conditional on the drawn subsets (first verdict) or approximately include them (second); the unit-norm k-NN gain at low labels depends on the choice `k = min(20, per-class count)`, which was fixed in advance but not varied; the hyperparameters were cross-validated within each tiny subset and are themselves noisy (selected values are in the per-lambda notes).

## Next steps

- Cosine k-NN is the natural reader at low labels (it is the `constant` condition); whether its advantage over Euclidean k-NN at 50 labels also holds for other embedding methods is not tested here.
- The confidence-like reading of the radius is tested more directly by selective classification (radius as a confidence score), not by randomization; see the other radius-usage notes.
- Training-seed variability: only 3 seeds per lambda; more seeds would be needed to bound the lambda trend of the k-NN effects.
