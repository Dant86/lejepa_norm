- **Backbone:** ResNet-18 from scratch ([HF architecture reference](https://huggingface.co/timm/resnet18.a1_in1k), weights not used); `last.pt` of `radius_marginal_lam{0.02,0.05,0.1}_s{0,1,2}` (Variant A). z only.

## Question

In `radius_aug_regression` the augmentation parameters explained only 6-13% of the within-image variance of `log ||z||`, yet the leftover radius still predicted whether the probe was right on a view. Is that leftover the view's direction, or the content of the crop? Predictions V1-V4 are in `preregistration.md`.

## Setup (deviations from the base config)

Same nine checkpoints, dumps, 15-covariate within-image model, probe and image-bootstrap (1000 replicates, 5 image folds) as `radius_aug_regression`. New blocks: *direction* = the view's angle to the mean direction of the image's other 7 views, and a cross-fitted ridge prediction of the demeaned radius from the within-image demeaned unit direction (penalty chosen among four values by held-out R2); *content* = luminance, luminance sd, mean gradient magnitude and color shift of the cropped raw pixels (before jitter, blur, flip).

Deviations from the pre-registration: (1) V2 and V4 named no number of checkpoints; I used the same 7 of 9 as V1 and V3 (V4 is evaluated on lambda means and seeds, see the table). (2) The ridge covariate is cross-fitted on the same folds as the second-stage cross-validation, so the second-stage held-out gains carry mild stacking optimism; the ridge's own held-out R2 (`ridge CV R2`) is the clean number. (3) The direction map is linear.

## Interpretation

- **The within-image radius is mostly direction (V1 holds in 9/9).** A linear map from the view's direction explains 34-73% of the within-image variance of `log ||z||` on held-out images (`ridge CV R2`), and adding it to the augmentation parameters raises held-out R2 from 0.06-0.13 to 0.46-0.75. The parameters add at most 0.014 to direction alone. The view spread is not small (18-24 degrees between two views of an image, against about 89 between images), so a view's radius is largely a function of where on the sphere it falls.
- **Crop content adds nothing (V2 holds in 9/9).** Gains are at most 0.001, with or without direction. The crop's luminance, contrast, edges and color shift are not what the radius tracks.
- **The correctness signal of the radius is carried by direction (V3 holds in 9/9, by the 0.53 rule).** The raw within-image radius separates correct from wrong views with AUC 0.565-0.580; after removing parameters it is 0.549-0.563, after removing direction 0.499-0.517. The 0.53 threshold is generous: with 95% intervals, 5 of 9 checkpoints include 0.5 after parameters, direction and content; the other four (lambda 0.05 s0, s1 and lambda 0.1 s0, s2) keep a small residual (AUC 0.514-0.522).
- **V4 fails, and the sign is opposite to the prediction.** The direction gain falls with lambda (0.63-0.66, then 0.42-0.46, then 0.33-0.36), in every seed. The spread of the views grows with lambda while the linear map explains less of it. I have not tested why; a candidate is that the radius is a nonlinear function of direction and the linear ridge captures less of it as the spread widens, which would also fit the small residual AUC that appears only at lambda 0.05 and 0.1. That is a hypothesis, not a result.
- **What this does and does not say.** The radius and the direction are two coordinates of the same output z, so "the radius is mostly direction" means the radius is nearly a function of the view's position on the sphere, not an independent per-view quantity. It fits the earlier finding that the radius carries no class information beyond direction, and it explains the earlier finding that the highest-radius view beats the lowest: the radius is a readout of direction-linked reliability, which is not a crop-size or blur effect (the previous note) and not crop content (this one). It does not show that the radius causes anything, and it does not show the relation is specific to SIGReg: the same-view R2 of the radius on direction is already generic (random init, VICReg, supervised).
- **Limits.** Global views only (crop scale 0.3-1.0), one dataset, one backbone, three seeds per lambda, a linear direction map.

## Next steps

1. Replace the linear direction map by a nonlinear one (kernel ridge or a small MLP on the direction) and see whether the lambda trend of the gain and the residual AUC at lambda 0.05 and 0.1 disappear.
2. Run the same within-image analysis on the E2 baseline checkpoints (random init, VICReg, supervised) to see whether "within-image radius is mostly direction" is specific to SIGReg.
3. Use the result for the powered view-selection test: whether dropping the lowest-radius views helps beyond dropping the views farthest from the image's mean direction.
