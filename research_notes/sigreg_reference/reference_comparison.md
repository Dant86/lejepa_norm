## Question
Does our SIGReg (`lejepa_norm.losses.sigreg`: Epps–Pulley, 1024 random slices,
17-point trapezoid on `[0, 3]`, scaled by N, fp32) agree with the official
implementation, in value **and** in scaling conventions? Specifically: (a) is the
statistic multiplied by N, and which N; (b) integration domain, nodes, weight;
(c) how slices are drawn and averaged; (d) the invariance loss (mean vs sum, which
views, detached or not); (e) how lambda combines the terms. These conventions set
what `lambda = 0.05` means.

## Setup (deviations from the base config)
- No training. `compare_sigreg.py` (this folder) imports the official `lejepa`
  package from `--official-dir` and reads the training example's `SIGReg` class and
  its `inv_loss =` / `lejepa_loss =` lines from the clone's `MINIMAL.md` at run time
  (the example's hard-coded `device="cuda"` is replaced by `"cpu"`; nothing else is
  changed). CPU, fp32, torch 2.14.1.
- **Same directions, no slice noise.** The official `SlicingUnivariateTest` draws a
  `(d, K)` standard normal matrix from a generator seeded with its step counter (0
  on a fresh module). Our `SIGReg` was given a `torch.Generator` seeded 0, which
  draws the same matrix. Every comparison uses a fresh official module and a fresh
  seeded generator, so both implementations see the same samples **and** the same
  slice directions. The training example (`MINIMAL.md`) draws from the global RNG,
  so it is called right after `torch.manual_seed(0)`.
- Inputs, `d = 256`, all affine maps of N(0, I) samples (seed 1): N(0, I);
  anisotropic (coordinate variances evenly spaced in `[0.25, 1.75]`, trace `d`);
  collapsed (only the first 16 coordinates non-zero); radius x1.1 (`1.1 z`); mean
  shift (`z + 0.1` on every coordinate). Sample sizes N = 256, 1536 (= our
  6 views x 256 images) and 6144.
- Training layout: `V = 6` views x `B = 256` images, view `v` of image `i` is
  `sqrt(1 - s^2) c_i + s e_vi` (then the input's affine map), so every view has the
  same marginal and `s` sets how similar the views of one image are (`s = 1`
  independent, `s = 0` identical).
- Noise floor: 10 repeats per N with fresh samples and fresh directions (shared by
  both implementations).

## Interpretation
**On identical 2D inputs the two statistics agree to float32 rounding.** The
relative difference is at most 6e-7 across all inputs, all three N and every
noise-floor repeat. The quadrature implies the same noise floor (1.052464). The
only difference that matters is **which samples go into one statistic**: ours pools
all views into one sample, while the official training example and the paper
compute one statistic per view and average them.

Conventions in the official code at `c293d29` (paths relative to the repo root,
line numbers at that commit; short quotes are from rbalestr-lab/lejepa, CC BY-NC
4.0):

| Item | Official | Ours | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) N factor | `lejepa/univariate/epps_pulley.py` L81 `N = x.size(-2)`, L99 `(err @ self.weights) * N * self.world_size`. Under DDP the ECF is all-reduced (L92–93), so N is the global per-call batch. The training example shapes projections as `(V, N, D)` (`MINIMAL.md` L93) and scales by `proj.size(-2)` (L74), which is the **per-view batch** B. The paper does the same: `sigreg = mean(SIGReg(emb, global_step) for emb in a_emb)` (Sec. 5.1 listing). | Multiplied by N = V·B = 1536 on the **concatenated** views | **Differs** |
| (b) Quadrature | `epps_pulley.py` L72–78: `linspace(0, t_max=3, 17)`, weights `2·dt` inside and `dt` at the ends (doubled trapezoid on `[0, 3]`, which equals `[-3, 3]` by symmetry), times weight `exp(-t²/2)`; target CF `exp(-t²/2)` | Same grid, weights and weight function | Match (floor 1.052464 in both; exact integral 1.052659) |
| (c) Slices | `lejepa/multivariate/slicing.py` L131–145: Gaussian `(d, K)` matrix with columns normalized to unit length, new directions every call (generator seeded by a step counter synced across ranks), mean over slices and any leading dims (L148–149). | (d) Invariance | `MINIMAL.md` L172 `inv_loss = (proj.mean(0) - proj).square().mean()`: mean over views, images and coordinates, center not detached. The center is the mean of all views because the example has no local views. In the paper's listing the center is the mean of the **global** views, applied to all views. | Center is the mean of the global views, not detached, mean over views/images/coordinates | Match (paper definition; identical values and gradients when all views are global) |

Side note: the README quick start passes `EppsPulley(num_points=17)` (README L108),
but the constructor argument is `n_points` (`epps_pulley.py` L63), so that snippet
fails as written. The script uses `n_points`.

**Why the view pooling matters.** It changes two things:

1. **Scale.** Above the noise floor the statistic is linear in N (the
   `(ours - floor) / N` column is flat from N = 256 to 6144). Pooling 6 views
   multiplies the distribution-mismatch term by about V = 6: we measured 5.6–6.0
   with independent views (radius, mean shift). So our `λ = 0.05` corresponds to
   an official `λ ≈ 0.23–0.24`, and the official `λ = 0.05` corresponds to ours
   at `λ ≈ 0.009`.
2. **Structure, which no λ can fix.** The views of one image are dependent, so the
   pooled sample is not i.i.d. With exactly Gaussian per-view marginals, the
   per-view statistic stays at the floor (≈1.0). The pooled one rises with
   cross-view similarity: 1.01 (independent), 3.87 (`s = 0.5`), 5.94 (`s = 0.1`)
   and 6.06 = V × floor (identical views). So our SIGReg **penalizes agreement
   between views of the same image**. That is exactly what the invariance term
   rewards, so ours adds a pull against invariance that the official objective
   does not have. Under strong invariance it is the dominant part of our SIGReg
   value.

**Recommendation (separate PR; `src/` is not edited on this branch).**
- Compute SIGReg **per view**: for `z` of shape `(V, B, d)`, compute the ECF over
  the B images of each view, scale by N = B (keep the N factor, both
  implementations have it), and average over views and slices. Global and local
  views are all included, as in the paper. Sharing one direction matrix across views
  in a call matches `MINIMAL.md`. The `ours per view` column shows that our current
  `SIGReg` applied view by view already reproduces the official example exactly, so
  the fix is a reshape in `LeJEPALoss` (or a batched `SIGReg.__call__` over a
  leading view dimension), plus a test that identical views give the per-view floor
  (≈1.05), not V × floor.
- With that change, `λ = 0.05` means the same as in the paper; keep it. If the
  concatenated version is kept for any reason, the closest match to the official
  `λ = 0.05` is `λ ≈ 0.009`, but the view-correlation penalty remains.
- Invariance loss and the λ combination: no change.
- Update the Losses section of the Technical Design ("applied to the
  concatenation of all views") and the `losses/lejepa.py` module docstring.
